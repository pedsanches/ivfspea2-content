\begin{abstract}
In vitro fertilization (IVF) intensification has been adapted separately to SPEA2, NSGA-II, and NSGA-III. Whether these couplings behave alike under a common protocol has not been tested; we show that they do not. Across 51 synthetic benchmark instances with two and three objectives, IVF/SPEA2 improves its host broadly and consistently, IVF/NSGA-III helps in a narrower set of cases, and IVF/NSGA-II is mostly neutral---the same ordering appears in both endpoint and search-dynamics analyses. The benefit of IVF therefore depends on the host: it is largest when the host's selection provides a fine-grained, directionally informative signal.

\keywords{Multi-objective optimization \and Memetic algorithms \and Intensification operators \and Local search \and Empirical comparison}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Adding local search to a multi-objective evolutionary algorithm (MOEA) is a common way to sharpen its results: a short burst of intensification refines promising solutions without replacing the underlying algorithm, which we call the \emph{host}. Whether such intensification helps, however, depends not only on its internal mechanics but also on the selection structure of the host into which it is inserted. A useful intensification step needs directional feedback from the host: information about where additional search effort is most likely to pay off.

The IVF family provides a natural case study for this question. IVF is a short-cycle intensification operator that selects a subset of top-ranked individuals as \emph{mothers}, pairs each with a \emph{father} drawn from the population, generates intensified offspring through a small number of internal reproduction cycles, and re-injects them into the host population. It was first proposed as a multi-objective hybrid inspired by in vitro fertilization~\cite{camilo-junior2011}. It was subsequently adapted as an operator for NSGA-II~\cite{sampaio2017ivf}, extended to many-objective settings~\cite{sampaio2019ivf}, specialized for NSGA-III~\cite{Sampaio2024}, and most recently redesigned for SPEA2~\cite{zambrano2026ivfspea2}. What changes across hosts is the realization of that core, including how mothers and fathers are chosen, how continuation is decided, and how the IVF budget is integrated with the host generation.

The broader literature on hybrid and memetic MOEAs---which combine global evolutionary search with local refinement---already suggests that intensification should be judged by how it couples to the host search process rather than by intensification strength alone. Classical memetic MOEAs such as \mbox{M-PAES}~\cite{knowles2000memetic}, component-wise MOEA design studies~\cite{bezerra2016automatic}, and hybrid-metaheuristic taxonomies and surveys~\cite{talbi2002taxonomy,blum2011hybrid} all point to the same design question: which host properties make a given intensification module useful? More recent mating-policy work reinforces this view by showing that structured pairing strategies can materially change multi-objective search behaviour~\cite{Lu2025}. At the operator level, IVF's generate-and-select cycle is a multi-objective descendant of soft brood selection~\cite{tackett1994brood}, which produces several offspring per mating and keeps the best.

We study IVF across the three hosts for which a realization has been proposed~\cite{sampaio2017ivf,Sampaio2024,zambrano2026ivfspea2}: SPEA2~\cite{zitzler2001spea2}, NSGA-II~\cite{deb2002nsga2}, and NSGA-III~\cite{deb2014nsga3}. These hosts differ in the kind of signal they provide during survival and diversity maintenance: SPEA2 combines strength and density, NSGA-II relies on non-dominated sorting plus crowding distance, and NSGA-III organizes selection around reference-point niches. IVF couplings for reference-vector or decomposition algorithms such as RVEA~\cite{cheng2016rvea} or MOEA/D~\cite{zhang2007moead} have not been proposed, and constructing them is left to future work.

\paragraph{Research question.}
Does the benefit of IVF depend on the host's selection mechanism, and if so, which structural property of the host---dominance strength, crowding, or reference-point niching---best explains the observed coupling?

\paragraph{Hypothesis H.}
IVF gains are strongest when the host ranks the non-dominated solutions in a \emph{fine-grained, direction-aware} way, because IVF draws its mothers from the top of that ranking and needs it to tell good intensification seeds apart. A host whose selection only spreads solutions out evenly---an isotropic crowding signal---should show weak synergy, whereas a host that points search toward specific regions through reference-point niches should fall in between, with gains that depend on front geometry and dimensionality.

\paragraph{What we compare.}
The unit of comparison is the \emph{host + host-specific IVF realization} pipeline: we compare three couplings from the literature under a unified benchmark and analysis protocol. Our conclusions therefore concern comparative compatibility patterns across existing pipelines, not the isolation of a pure host-mechanism effect under a single identical IVF implementation transplanted unchanged across hosts.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Base Algorithms}
\label{sec:base_algos}

SPEA2~\cite{zitzler2001spea2} combines dominance strength and density in an external-archive setting, so its selection pressure carries information about both solution quality and local occupancy. NSGA-II~\cite{deb2002nsga2} relies on non-dominated sorting plus crowding distance, which preserves spread but offers mostly isotropic feedback about sparsity. NSGA-III~\cite{deb2014nsga3} adds reference-point association and niche preservation, turning diversity maintenance into an explicit directional signal about underserved regions.

\subsection{The IVF Operator Family}
\label{sec:ivf_family}

Across IVF variants, the shared core is short-cycle intensification: a subset of promising \emph{mothers} (top-ranked candidates under the host's selection order) is paired with \emph{fathers} (a second set of parents drawn from the same population), intensified offspring are generated by a crossover operator, and the cycle repeats for a limited number of rounds while a continuation criterion is satisfied~\cite{sampaio2017ivf,sampaio2019ivf,Sampaio2024,zambrano2026ivfspea2}. Mothers and fathers are ordinary individuals with the same representation; the labels denote only a role within a pairing, so an individual may serve as either across cycles. What changes across hosts is how mothers and fathers are chosen, how continuation is judged, and how the host absorbs the intensified offspring.

\section{Host-Specific IVF Realizations}
\label{sec:adaptations}

The three implementations studied here inherit the shared macro-structure summarized in Section~\ref{sec:ivf_family}. They differ in four design axes: father selection, offspring generation, continuation criterion, and activation budget. Mother selection always follows the host's own primary ordering---a scalar fitness for SPEA2 and Pareto rank for NSGA-II and NSGA-III. Table~\ref{tab:adaptations} gives the full breakdown.

\subsection{IVF/SPEA2}
\label{sec:ivfspea2}

IVF/SPEA2 v2 is the implementation introduced in~\cite{zambrano2026ivfspea2} and adopted here. It selects mothers from the best portion of the combined population--archive ranking and applies two design changes. First, each mother is paired with a distinct father chosen to lie far from her in objective space---maximizing the dissimilarity between the two parents---and then confirmed by a binary tournament on SPEA2 fitness, so the crossover spans a wide region while still favoring high-quality parents. Second, a cycle continues only while the \emph{mean} fitness of the whole intensified set keeps improving, rather than requiring any single offspring to beat its father. Offspring are produced with SBX~\cite{deb1995simulated} ($\eta_c=20$), and activation is governed by a cumulative budget gate.

\subsection{IVF/NSGA-II}
\label{sec:ivfnsgaii}

IVF/NSGA-II follows the operator introduced in~\cite{sampaio2017ivf}. Mothers are taken from the top-ranked portion of the population, while fathers are sampled uniformly at random from the whole population rather than by dissimilarity. Offspring are generated by SBX ($\eta_c=15$). A cycle continues while the best offspring improves on a designated objective, the original best-on-objective rule, and activation is decided probabilistically per generation.

\subsection{IVF/NSGA-III}
\label{sec:ivfnsgaiii}

IVF/NSGA-III adopts the host-specific adaptation of~\cite{Sampaio2024}. Mothers are again drawn from the top-ranked portion of the population and fathers are sampled uniformly at random, but offspring are produced by differential evolution (DE)~\cite{storn1997de} in its current-to-best form rather than by SBX: each child perturbs a base solution along the scaled difference of two others, biased towards the current best. Continuation is governed by niche proximity, so a cycle is kept while it improves occupation of the NSGA-III reference directions. Activation is governed by a cumulative rate gate.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

All experiments were conducted in MATLAB~\texttt{R2025b Update 5} using PlatEMO version~\texttt{4.6}~\cite{tian2017platemo}. We considered 51 synthetic benchmark instances: 28 bi-objective instances from ZDT~\cite{zitzler2000zdt}, DTLZ~\cite{deb2005dtlz}, WFG~\cite{huband2006wfg}, and MaF~\cite{cheng2017maf}; and 23 tri-objective instances from DTLZ, WFG, and MaF. This yields 28 instances with $M=2$ and 23 instances with $M=3$. Each configuration was executed for 30 runs with population size $N=100$ and stopping criterion $\mathrm{maxFE}=100{,}000$. All hosts use the standard generational model: every generation produces $N$ offspring through the host's crossover and mutation, after which environmental selection retains $N$ survivors. Per-instance outcomes for every host pair are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap}, and the full per-technique median/IQR tables for all instances are provided in the archived artifact.

\paragraph{Evaluation budget.}
The comparison is evaluation-normalized rather than runtime-normalized. In all three host-specific IVF realizations, evaluations spent inside IVF are debited from the host's generation budget. The IVF phase consumes at most $N$ internal evaluations per activated generation, and the remaining host variation budget is reduced to \(N - \mathrm{FE}_{\mathrm{IVF,gen}}\). Consequently, the nominal generation budget remains fixed at $N$, although wall-clock overhead still differs across hosts because the IVF realizations use different internal selection, continuation, and offspring-generation routines.

\paragraph{Metrics and references.}
Final IGD~\cite{zitzler1999igd} and HV~\cite{zitzler2003hv} were used as endpoint metrics. For the synthetic benchmarks, both were taken from PlatEMO's built-in metric outputs. Each problem instance constructs a problem-specific reference set through \texttt{Problem.GetOptimum(10000)} before optimization. IGD is then computed as the mean distance from this 10{,}000-point reference-front sample to the final nondominated approximation, whereas HV uses the same problem-specific optimum set to determine the normalization bounds in PlatEMO's standard HV implementation, after which the reference point is the all-ones vector in normalized objective space. The runs were executed on an Intel 13th Gen Core i5-13600KF CPU with 62 GiB RAM running Ubuntu 24.04.4 LTS, and the processed CSV files, raw metric traces, and analysis scripts used to generate the reported tables and figures are archived at \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19637623}.

\paragraph{Pairing and endpoint inference.}
The three host comparisons do not share the same pairing status. IVF/NSGA-II versus NSGA-II and IVF/NSGA-III versus NSGA-III use strict run-ID pairing because each IVF track shares the same run range as its baseline track (4001--4030 and 5001--5030, respectively). IVF/SPEA2 versus SPEA2 does not share absolute run IDs (3001--3030 versus 1--30); for this block we therefore report the deterministic matched-order analysis used in the original version together with an unpaired robustness analysis in the supplementary artifact. Endpoint comparisons use the Wilcoxon signed-rank test~\cite{wilcoxon1945} on strictly paired blocks and an unpaired robustness analysis on the order-aligned block; Vargha--Delaney $A_{12}$~\cite{vargha2000a12} is reported throughout as effect size.

\paragraph{Multiplicity control.}
We use Benjamini--Hochberg (BH) false-discovery-rate control~\cite{benjamini1995bh} rather than a family-wise procedure such as Bonferroni--Holm because the goal is to characterize broad comparative patterns across many benchmark instances without the severe power loss that arises under dozens of simultaneous tests. For endpoint analyses, BH is applied separately within each \emph{(host comparison, metric)} family across the 51 instance-level hypotheses. For dynamic per-run trajectory summaries, BH is applied separately within each summary metric across the 18 host-instance hypotheses in the dynamic block. For empirical cumulative distribution function (ECDF) differences, BH is applied across the 18 host-instance Kolmogorov--Smirnov tests in the ECDF block.

\paragraph{Parameter provenance.}
No additional instance-wise retuning was performed for this host-comparison study. Each pipeline used the fixed parameter setting of its cited canonical implementation throughout the 51-instance evaluation: IVF/SPEA2 v2 used $C=0.12$, $R=0.225$, $M_{\mathrm{mut}}=0.3$, $V=0.1$, and \textit{Cycles} = 2; IVF/NSGA-II used $R=0.5$, $C=0.07$, and \textit{Cycles} = 5; and IVF/NSGA-III used \textit{ivf\_rate}=0.10, $C=0.10$, and \textit{Cycles} = 5.

\paragraph{Dynamic block.}
To complement the 51-instance endpoint analysis with trajectory-level evidence, we also analyze a smaller dynamic block defined by a deterministic six-instance protocol: DTLZ5 ($M=2$), MaF1 ($M=2$), WFG8 ($M=2$), ZDT4 ($M=2$), DTLZ5 ($M=3$), and WFG2 ($M=2$). These configurations were rerun with 100 saved checkpoints. The dynamic block is intended as mechanism-oriented diagnostic evidence rather than as a second confirmatory benchmark of the same scope as the 51-instance endpoint analysis.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

At the aggregate level, the three pipelines already separate by a large margin (Fig.~\ref{fig:a12_strip}). The ranking is clear: IVF/SPEA2's effect-size distribution is shifted furthest toward IVF benefit, IVF/NSGA-III's is positive but weaker, and \mbox{IVF/NSGA-II's} stays close to neutrality. The median oriented effect size is about 0.75 for IVF/SPEA2, 0.60 for IVF/NSGA-III, and 0.51 for IVF/NSGA-II, which indicates that host compatibility manifests in effect-size magnitude, not only in win counts. Sections~\ref{sec:results_geometry} and~\ref{sec:results_dynamic} later show that the same ranking reappears in geometry-stratified endpoint summaries and in all-suite trajectory aggregates rather than only at the final checkpoint.

\subsection{Per-Host Compatibility}
\label{sec:results_per_host}

Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap} disaggregates the aggregate picture to the instance level. The same ranking remains visible, and the figure additionally shows which benchmark families drive it. \subsection{Effect of Pareto-Front Geometry}
\label{sec:results_geometry}

Stratifying by Pareto-front geometry clarifies the source of the endpoint gains. Table~\ref{tab:hosts_geometry_summary} splits the 51 instances into \emph{regular} (convex, concave, linear) and \emph{irregular} (disconnected, degenerate) Pareto-front geometries. IVF/SPEA2 remains strongest in both strata but is clearly stronger on regular fronts: +28/=11/-1 in IGD and +30/=8/-2 in HV, versus +5/=4/-2 and +7/=2/-2 on irregular fronts. IVF/NSGA-III shows the same directional drop, from +20/=20/-0 and +18/=20/-2 on regular fronts to +2/=9/-0 and +3/=8/-0 on irregular fronts. IVF/NSGA-II remains weak throughout and becomes effectively non-improving on irregular fronts.

This stratified pattern is consistent with the directional-feedback interpretation, but it is not yet a strong interaction result in the statistical sense. The permutation tests on $A_{12}^{\mathrm{IVF}}$ do not detect a robust host$\times$geometry interaction in either metric under either geometry encoding (all $p \geq 0.356$), so the evidence here should be read as descriptive stratification rather than as confirmatory interaction testing.

\subsection{Evidence During the Search}
\label{sec:results_dynamic}

To complement the endpoint analysis with temporal evidence, we use two dynamic views. The first is an all-suite aggregate over the reconstructed 51-instance trace cohort; the second is the smaller six-instance diagnostic block used for mechanism-oriented visualization. The aggregate view serves as the primary dynamic evidence; the six-instance traces serve as diagnostic case studies.

For the all-suite aggregate, every run is transformed into a normalized IGD progress gap. The gap is zero at the pair-specific initial state and one at the best final value attained by either algorithm on that instance. Figure~\ref{fig:dynamic_aggregate} then tracks the fraction of runs whose cumulative-best IGD reaches a normalized gap of at most $\tau=0.1$ as a function of FE / maxFE. This removes the instance-selection issue of the six-case block and still preserves the same ordering at the trajectory level: in Figure~\ref{fig:dynamic_aggregate} the IVF/SPEA2 solid curve rises above its dashed SPEA2 baseline within the first 20\% of the evaluation budget and maintains separation to the end, IVF/NSGA-III's curves overlap or cross the base for much of the budget, and \mbox{IVF/NSGA-II's} curves nearly coincide with a small late-stage separation.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Across all three analyses---endpoint, dimensional, and dynamic---the evidence supports Hypothesis~H at the pipeline level (Figs.~\ref{fig:a12_strip}--\ref{fig:dynamic_aggregate}; Table~\ref{tab:hosts_geometry_summary}), but it does not establish directional feedback as the sole causal mechanism. The common IVF core is shared across hosts, yet the host-specific realizations also differ in father selection, reproduction, continuation, and activation policy. The present evidence therefore supports a comparative reading: among the canonical pipelines considered here, IVF/SPEA2 couples most effectively to its host, IVF/NSGA-III remains conditionally beneficial, and IVF/NSGA-II is weakest overall, with only modest dynamic gains in some regular strata.

A plausible interpretation is that hosts providing more explicit directional information in objective space enable the IVF core to convert local intensification into offspring that survive environmental selection. This interpretation is consistent with the observed ranking, but it should still be read as a mechanistic account supported by the current evidence rather than as a mechanism already isolated experimentally. Its most useful implication is predictive: if the interpretation is correct, the strength of IVF should vary not only with the host but also with Pareto-front geometry. The geometry-stratified summaries are directionally consistent with that prediction---regular fronts favor IVF/SPEA2 most strongly and weaken both IVF/NSGA-III and IVF/NSGA-II---but the permutation tests do not yet show a strong host$\times$geometry interaction, so this part of the evidence remains suggestive rather than definitive.

Several threats to validity remain relevant. First, the comparison is evaluation-normalized only if the documented FE accounting is accepted; wall-clock overhead still differs across hosts. Second, the inference is strongest for strict run-ID pairing and weaker for the order-aligned IVF/SPEA2 versus SPEA2 block, which is why the robustness analysis is reported separately. Third, any parameter adjustment performed on the reported benchmark suite would blur the distinction between host compatibility and problem-specific calibration. Fourth, the pipelines differ not only in host but also in variation operator---SBX for IVF/SPEA2 and IVF/NSGA-II, differential evolution for IVF/NSGA-III---and in selection pressure and diversity maintenance, so the measured effect cannot be attributed to the host's selection mechanism in isolation. Fifth, improvement is reported relative to each base host, and part of the gain may reflect how much room a weaker baseline leaves rather than the IVF core itself; absolute IGD and HV values for all hosts are included in the artifact for direct inspection. These limitations do not erase the pipeline-level result, but they bound the causal interpretation that can be attached to it; separating host compatibility from operator, selection-pressure, and baseline-strength effects is the aim of the controlled cross-host experiments left to future work.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We compared three IVF pipelines rather than a single identical IVF implementation transplanted across hosts. Under that scope, the evidence is consistent: IVF/SPEA2 shows the strongest and most stable gains, IVF/NSGA-III is a conditional second host, and IVF/NSGA-II is largely neutral. The results therefore support the conclusion that existing IVF couplings are not interchangeable across hosts.

A broader claim about operator-host compatibility as a mechanism remains plausible but provisional. It is supported by the observed pipeline ordering, by the all-suite dynamic aggregate and the diagnostic case studies, and by suggestive geometry-stratified patterns, but it is not yet isolated from differences among the host-specific IVF realizations themselves and the geometry interaction tests are not yet significant. Future work should therefore separate mechanism from implementation more cleanly through cross-host transplant experiments, broader geometry-stratified analyses, and higher-dimensional benchmarks.
