\begin{abstract}
In vitro fertilization (IVF) intensification has been adapted separately to SPEA2, NSGA-II, and NSGA-III. Whether these couplings behave alike under a common protocol has not been tested; we show that they do not. Across 51 synthetic benchmark instances with two and three objectives, IVF/SPEA2 improves its host in most instances, IVF/NSGA-III helps in fewer cases, and IVF/NSGA-II stays close to neutral; endpoint and search-dynamics analyses give the same ordering. IVF benefit therefore depends on the host: it is largest when the host's selection provides a fine-grained, directionally informative signal.

\keywords{Memetic algorithms \and Local search \and Empirical benchmarking \and Search dynamics \and Evolutionary computation}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A local-search module that helps one Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithm (MOEA) need not help another. Its effect depends not only on its own reproduction rule but also on the selection and diversity machinery into which it is inserted. We use \emph{host} in this hybrid-algorithm sense: the base MOEA that embeds the module and performs survival selection. The practical question is therefore not whether IVF is strong in isolation, but which hosts give IVF useful direction for extra search effort.

The IVF family is a useful test case for this question. IVF is a short-cycle intensification operator that selects a subset of top-ranked individuals as \emph{mothers}, pairs each with a \emph{father} drawn from the population, generates intensified offspring through a small number of internal reproduction cycles, and re-injects them into the host population. It was first proposed as a multi-objective hybrid inspired by in vitro fertilization~\cite{camilo-junior2011}. It was subsequently adapted as an operator for NSGA-II~\cite{sampaio2017ivf}, extended to many-objective settings~\cite{sampaio2019ivf}, specialized for NSGA-III~\cite{Sampaio2024}, and most recently redesigned for SPEA2~\cite{zambrano2026ivfspea2}. What changes across hosts is the realization of that core, including how mothers and fathers are chosen, how continuation is decided, and how the IVF budget is integrated with the host generation.

Hybrid and memetic MOEA work suggests that embedded improvement steps should be judged by how they couple to the host search process. Here, \emph{hybrid} means combining search components, while \emph{memetic} emphasizes local improvement inside an evolutionary algorithm. Classical memetic MOEAs such as \mbox{M-PAES}~\cite{knowles2000memetic}, studies of MOEA components~\cite{bezerra2016automatic}, and surveys of hybrid metaheuristics~\cite{talbi2002taxonomy,blum2011hybrid} raise the same design question: which host properties make a given module useful? Outside the IVF line initiated by Camilo-Junior and Yamanaka~\cite{camilo-junior2011}, Tackett and Carmi's soft brood selection~\cite{tackett1994brood} is a related brood-selection mechanism: it generates multiple offspring from a mating event and keeps promising brood members. IVF differs in that it chooses mothers from the host order, repeats only while a host-specific continuation rule holds, and charges the resulting offspring to a fixed evaluation budget. Recent mating-policy results also show that structured pairing strategies can change multi-objective search behaviour~\cite{Lu2025}.

We study IVF across the three hosts for which a realization has been proposed~\cite{sampaio2017ivf,Sampaio2024,zambrano2026ivfspea2}: SPEA2~\cite{zitzler2001spea2}, NSGA-II~\cite{deb2002nsga2}, and NSGA-III~\cite{deb2014nsga3}. These hosts differ in the kind of signal they provide during survival and diversity maintenance: dominance strength, crowding, or reference-point niching. SPEA2 combines strength and density, NSGA-II relies on non-dominated sorting plus crowding distance, and NSGA-III uses reference-point niches to organize selection.

\paragraph{Research question.}
Does the benefit of IVF depend on the host's selection mechanism, and if so, which structural property of the host best explains the observed coupling?

\paragraph{Hypothesis H.}
IVF should help most when the host gives mother selection a fine, directional ordering among good solutions. SPEA2's strength-density fitness should provide that signal. NSGA-II's crowding distance mainly identifies sparse regions, so it should give weaker guidance. NSGA-III's reference-point niches provide directional guidance too, but that guidance may weaken as front geometry or objective count changes.

\paragraph{What we compare.}
The unit of comparison is the \emph{host + host-specific IVF realization} pipeline: we compare three couplings from the literature under a unified benchmark and analysis protocol. We deliberately restrict the study to IVF couplings that already exist, rather than designing new host pairings for this evaluation. Our conclusions therefore concern comparative compatibility patterns across existing pipelines, not the isolation of a pure host-mechanism effect under a single identical IVF implementation transplanted unchanged across hosts.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Base Algorithms}
\label{sec:base_algos}

SPEA2~\cite{zitzler2001spea2} combines dominance strength and density in an external-archive setting, so its selection pressure carries information about both solution quality and local occupancy. NSGA-II~\cite{deb2002nsga2} relies on non-dominated sorting plus crowding distance, which preserves spread but offers mostly isotropic feedback about sparsity. NSGA-III~\cite{deb2014nsga3} adds reference-point association and niche preservation, turning diversity maintenance into an explicit directional signal about underserved regions.

\subsection{The IVF Operator Family}
\label{sec:ivf_family}

Across IVF variants, the shared core is short-cycle intensification~\cite{camilo-junior2011}: a subset of promising \emph{mothers} (top-ranked candidates under the host's selection order) is paired with \emph{fathers} (a second set of parents drawn from the same population), intensified offspring are generated by a crossover operator, and the cycle repeats for a limited number of rounds while a continuation criterion is satisfied. Mothers and fathers are ordinary individuals with the same representation; the labels denote only a role within a pairing, so an individual may serve as either across cycles. What changes across hosts is how mothers and fathers are chosen, how continuation is judged, and how the host absorbs the intensified offspring~\cite{sampaio2017ivf,sampaio2019ivf,Sampaio2024,zambrano2026ivfspea2}.

\section{Host-Specific IVF Realizations}
\label{sec:adaptations}

The three implementations studied here inherit the shared macro-structure summarized in Section~\ref{sec:ivf_family}. They differ in four design axes: father selection, offspring generation, continuation criterion, and activation budget. Mother selection always follows the host's own primary ordering: a scalar fitness for SPEA2 and Pareto rank for NSGA-II and NSGA-III. Table~\ref{tab:adaptations} gives the full breakdown.

\subsection{IVF/SPEA2}
\label{sec:ivfspea2}

IVF/SPEA2 is the implementation introduced in~\cite{zambrano2026ivfspea2} and adopted here. It selects mothers from the best portion of the combined population--archive ranking and applies two design changes. First, each mother is paired with a distinct father chosen from distant candidates in objective space and then selected by a binary tournament on SPEA2 fitness; the crossover therefore spans a wide region while still favoring high-quality parents. Second, a cycle continues only while the \emph{mean} fitness of the whole intensified set keeps improving, rather than requiring any single offspring to beat its father. Offspring are produced with SBX~\cite{deb1995simulated}, using distribution index $\eta_c=20$, and activation is governed by a cumulative budget gate.

\subsection{IVF/NSGA-II}
\label{sec:ivfnsgaii}

IVF/NSGA-II follows the operator introduced in~\cite{sampaio2017ivf}. The collected set is ordered by NSGA-II rank and crowding distance; its best individual becomes the initial father, and the remaining collected individuals become mothers. Offspring are generated by SBX with distribution index $\eta_c=15$. A cycle continues while the best offspring improves on a designated objective, the original best-on-objective rule, and activation is decided probabilistically per generation.

\subsection{IVF/NSGA-III}
\label{sec:ivfnsgaiii}

IVF/NSGA-III adopts the host-specific adaptation of~\cite{Sampaio2024}. The initial father is the solution associated with the target niche of worst coverage, and mothers are neighbouring solutions collected around that target. Offspring are produced by Differential Evolution (DE)~\cite{storn1997de} in its current-to-best form rather than by SBX: each child perturbs a mother along the scaled difference toward the current father. Continuation is governed by niche proximity, so a cycle is kept while it improves occupation of the NSGA-III reference directions. Activation is governed by a cumulative rate gate.

\subsection{Comparison Unit}
\label{sec:comparison_unit}

Because the realizations differ in several components, compatibility claims here refer to the complete pipeline---that is, the host algorithm together with its host-specific IVF realization---rather than to a single IVF module held identical across hosts. The experimental protocol follows.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

All experiments were conducted in MATLAB~\texttt{R2025b Update 5} using PlatEMO version~\texttt{4.6}~\cite{tian2017platemo}. We considered 51 synthetic benchmark instances: 28 bi-objective instances from ZDT~\cite{zitzler2000zdt}, DTLZ~\cite{deb2005dtlz}, WFG~\cite{huband2006wfg}, and MaF~\cite{cheng2017maf}; and 23 tri-objective instances from DTLZ, WFG, and MaF. This yields 28 instances with $M=2$ and 23 instances with $M=3$. Each configuration was executed for 30 runs with population size $N=100$ and stopping criterion $\mathrm{maxFE}=100{,}000$. All compared algorithms are generational: after each IVF and/or host-variation phase, environmental selection reduces the parent--offspring union back to $N=100$. The remaining host offspring are produced by PlatEMO's standard genetic operator, which applies crossover and mutation; the IVF offspring generator is the host-specific one in Table~\ref{tab:adaptations}.

\paragraph{Fixed evaluation budget.}
The comparison is evaluation-normalized rather than runtime-normalized. In all three host-specific IVF realizations, evaluations spent inside IVF are debited from the host's generation budget. The IVF phase consumes at most $N$ internal evaluations per activated generation, and the remaining host variation budget is reduced to \(N - \mathrm{FE}_{\mathrm{IVF,gen}}\). Consequently, the nominal generation budget remains fixed at $N$, although wall-clock overhead still differs across hosts because the IVF realizations use different internal selection, continuation, and offspring-generation routines.

\paragraph{Endpoint metrics.}
Final IGD~\cite{zitzler1999igd} and HV~\cite{zitzler2003hv} were used as endpoint metrics. For the synthetic benchmarks, both were taken from PlatEMO's built-in metric outputs. Each problem instance constructs a problem-specific reference set through \texttt{Problem.GetOptimum(10000)} before optimization. IGD is then computed as the mean distance from this 10{,}000-point reference-front sample to the final nondominated approximation, whereas HV uses the same problem-specific optimum set to determine the normalization bounds in PlatEMO's standard HV implementation, after which the reference point is the all-ones vector in normalized objective space. The runs were executed on an Intel 13th Gen Core i5-13600KF CPU with 62 GiB RAM running Ubuntu 24.04.4 LTS, and the processed CSV files, raw metric traces, and analysis scripts used to generate the reported tables and figures are archived at \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20672828}.

\paragraph{Endpoint tests and pairing.}
The three host comparisons do not share the same pairing status. IVF/NSGA-II versus NSGA-II and IVF/NSGA-III versus NSGA-III use strict run-ID pairing because each IVF track shares the same run range as its baseline track (4001--4030 and 5001--5030, respectively). IVF/SPEA2 versus SPEA2 does not share absolute run IDs (3001--3030 versus 1--30); for this block we therefore report the deterministic matched-order analysis used in the original version together with an unpaired sensitivity analysis in the supplementary artifact. Endpoint comparisons use the Wilcoxon signed-rank test~\cite{wilcoxon1945} on strictly paired blocks and an unpaired sensitivity analysis on the order-aligned block; Vargha--Delaney $A_{12}$~\cite{vargha2000a12} is reported throughout as effect size.

\paragraph{Multiple comparisons.}
We use Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate control~\cite{benjamini1995bh} rather than a family-wise procedure such as Bonferroni--Holm because the goal is to characterize broad comparative patterns across many benchmark instances without making the correction overly conservative. For endpoint analyses, the Benjamini--Hochberg correction is applied separately within each \emph{(host comparison, metric)} family across the 51 instance-level hypotheses. For dynamic per-run trajectory summaries, the same correction is applied separately within each summary metric across the 18 host-instance hypotheses in the dynamic block. For empirical cumulative distribution differences, it is applied across the 18 host-instance Kolmogorov--Smirnov tests in the dynamic block.

\paragraph{Fixed parameter settings.}
No additional instance-wise retuning was performed for this host-comparison study. Each pipeline used the fixed parameter setting of its cited canonical implementation throughout the 51-instance evaluation: IVF/SPEA2 used $C=0.12$, $R=0.225$, $M_{\mathrm{mut}}=0.3$, $V=0.1$, and \textit{Cycles} = 2; IVF/NSGA-II used $R=0.5$, $C=0.07$, and \textit{Cycles} = 5; and IVF/NSGA-III used \textit{ivf\_rate}=0.10, $C=0.10$, and \textit{Cycles} = 5.

\paragraph{Search-dynamics data.}
For trajectory-level evidence, we also analyze a smaller dynamic block defined by a deterministic six-instance protocol: DTLZ5 ($M=2$), MaF1 ($M=2$), WFG8 ($M=2$), ZDT4 ($M=2$), DTLZ5 ($M=3$), and WFG2 ($M=2$). These configurations were rerun with 100 saved checkpoints. The dynamic block is intended as mechanism-oriented diagnostic evidence rather than as a second confirmatory benchmark of the same scope as the 51-instance endpoint analysis.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The aggregate effect sizes separate the three pipelines (Fig.~\ref{fig:a12_strip}). IVF/SPEA2's distribution shifts furthest toward IVF benefit, IVF/NSGA-III is positive but weaker, and IVF/NSGA-II stays close to neutrality. Median oriented effect sizes are about 0.75, 0.60, and 0.51, respectively, so compatibility appears in effect-size magnitude and not only in win counts. The same ranking also appears in geometry-stratified endpoint summaries and in all-suite trajectory aggregates rather than only at the final checkpoint.

\subsection{Instance-Level Outcomes}
\label{sec:results_per_host}

Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap} reports the same comparison at the instance level, identifying the IVF pipeline that won on each benchmark and the benchmark families behind the aggregate ranking. Under the aligned paired analysis visualized here, IVF/SPEA2 dominates the synthetic suite with +33/=15/-3 in IGD and +37/=10/-4 in HV over the 51 instances. IVF/NSGA-III improves fewer cases, with +22/=29/-0 in IGD and +21/=28/-2 in HV. IVF/NSGA-II is close to inert, with only +1/=48/-2 in IGD and +5/=43/-3 in HV. The pairing-aware sensitivity reanalysis leaves the IGD tallies unchanged and shifts the IVF/SPEA2 HV count by only one case (+38/=9/-4 under the unpaired primary test), so the host ranking is not an artifact of the alignment choice.

\subsection{Pareto-Front Geometry}
\label{sec:results_geometry}

Stratifying by Pareto-front geometry clarifies the source of the endpoint gains. Table~\ref{tab:hosts_geometry_summary} splits the 51 instances into \emph{regular} (convex, concave, linear) and \emph{irregular} (disconnected, degenerate) Pareto-front geometries. IVF/SPEA2 remains strongest in both strata but is stronger on regular fronts: +28/=11/-1 in IGD and +30/=8/-2 in HV, versus +5/=4/-2 and +7/=2/-2 on irregular fronts. IVF/NSGA-III also drops across strata: regular fronts give 20 wins, 20 ties, and no losses in IGD, and 18 wins, 20 ties, and 2 losses in HV. Irregular fronts give 2 wins, 9 ties, and no losses in IGD, and 3 wins, 8 ties, and no losses in HV. IVF/NSGA-II remains weak throughout and becomes effectively non-improving on irregular fronts.

This stratification matches the directional-feedback interpretation, but the interaction tests do not make it confirmatory. The permutation tests on $A_{12}^{\mathrm{IVF}}$ do not detect a host$\times$geometry interaction in either metric under either geometry encoding (all $p \geq 0.356$), so the evidence here should be read as descriptive stratification rather than as confirmatory interaction testing.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results support Hypothesis~H at the pipeline level (Figs.~\ref{fig:a12_strip}--\ref{fig:dynamic_aggregate}; Table~\ref{tab:hosts_geometry_summary}), but they do not establish directional feedback as the sole causal mechanism. The common IVF core is shared across hosts, yet the host-specific realizations also differ in father selection, reproduction, continuation, and activation policy. The evidence supports a comparative reading: among the canonical pipelines considered here, IVF/SPEA2 couples most effectively to its host, IVF/NSGA-III remains conditionally beneficial, and IVF/NSGA-II is weakest overall, with only modest dynamic gains in some regular strata.

One interpretation is that hosts providing more explicit directional information in objective space enable the IVF core to convert local intensification into offspring that survive environmental selection. This interpretation is consistent with the observed ranking, but it should still be read as a mechanistic account supported by the current evidence rather than as a mechanism already isolated experimentally. The implication is predictive: if the interpretation is correct, the strength of IVF should vary not only with the host but also with Pareto-front geometry. The geometry-stratified summaries match that prediction: regular fronts favor IVF/SPEA2 most strongly and weaken both IVF/NSGA-III and IVF/NSGA-II. The permutation tests, however, do not yet show a strong host$\times$geometry interaction, so this part of the evidence remains descriptive.

Four threats to validity remain relevant. First, variation operators are confounded with host choice: IVF/NSGA-III uses DE/current-to-best while the other two IVF realizations use SBX, and IVF/SPEA2 also mutates selected mothers under its cited setting. Some differences may therefore reflect the offspring generator rather than host selection alone. Second, relative IVF gains can be larger for a weaker baseline, so the absolute IGD/HV tables in the artifact should be read alongside the relative effect sizes. Third, the observed ranking could also reflect broader differences in selection pressure or diversity preservation, not only directional feedback. Fourth, the comparison is evaluation-normalized only if the documented FE accounting is accepted; wall-clock overhead still differs across hosts. These limitations do not invalidate the pipeline-level result, but they limit the strength of the causal interpretation that can be attached to it.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We compared three IVF pipelines rather than a single identical IVF implementation transplanted across hosts. Under that scope, the evidence is consistent: IVF/SPEA2 shows the strongest and most stable gains, IVF/NSGA-III is a conditional second host, and IVF/NSGA-II is largely neutral. The results therefore support the conclusion that existing IVF couplings are not interchangeable across hosts.

The mechanism-level claim remains provisional. The observed pipeline ordering, all-suite dynamic aggregate, diagnostic traces, and geometry-stratified summaries point in the same direction, but the experiments do not isolate host feedback from the host-specific IVF implementations, and the geometry interaction tests are not significant. Future work should therefore separate mechanism from implementation more cleanly through controlled factorial experiments over host, variation operator, IVF strategy, and budget policy; cross-host transplant experiments including newly designed RVEA or MOEA/D couplings; broader geometry-stratified analyses; and higher-dimensional benchmarks.
