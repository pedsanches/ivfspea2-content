\begin{abstract}
	Adaptive operator control often relies on reward signals accumulated
	during the run, yet for some operators a single keep-or-drop decision
	after a short warmup may suffice. We investigate whether static
	fitness-landscape features or early population dynamics better inform
	such a decision for an IVF-inspired intensification operator inside
	SPEA2. On 51 continuous multi-objective benchmark instances, a
	14-feature landscape descriptor provides moderate signal for ranking
	expected IGD gain (Random Forest leave-one-out $r_s\!=\!0.374$), but
	binary classification does not generalize across benchmark families.
	In contrast, mean archive turnover during the first 20\% of generations
	yields a stronger switching signal ($q_{\mathrm{BH}}\!=\!0.013$,
	$\hat{A}_{12}\!=\!0.849$); a single-threshold rule achieves balanced
	accuracy $0.754$ under leave-one-family-out cross-validation. A
	warmup-based controller using this signal eliminates all hypervolume
	losses on the 10 instances where IVF is unhelpful and records 34
	wins against baseline SPEA2, at the cost of forfeiting gains on
	9 beneficial instances concentrated in WFG.
	Our results suggest that, for this operator--host pair, static
	features serve as priors on gain magnitude whereas early dynamics are
	more actionable for binary switching.

	\keywords{Fitness landscape analysis \and Adaptive operator control \and
		Multi-objective optimization \and Evolutionary algorithms \and Population dynamics}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Whether a search operator helps a multi-objective evolutionary algorithm
depends on both the problem instance and the phase of the run. An
intensification step that accelerates convergence on one landscape may waste
evaluations on another.

Fitness landscape analysis (FLA) describes optimization problems through
numerical features of sampled search behavior and objective structure. In
single-objective continuous optimization, such descriptors have supported
algorithm selection with measurable gains over fixed
solvers~\cite{mersmann2011exploratory,kerschke2019automated,bischl2016aslib}.
Multi-objective extensions exist~\cite{liefooghe2018landscape}, but their
empirical use for operator-level decisions is limited.

Most FLA studies target full algorithm selection. Here we study a narrower
decision: after a warmup phase, should one intensification operator remain
active inside a fixed host algorithm? The question is adjacent to adaptive
operator selection~\cite{fialho2008adaptive,maturana2009adaptive} and
component-wise algorithm design~\cite{bezerra2016automatic}, but we focus on
one operator, one host algorithm, and one irreversible keep/drop decision
rather than repeated on-line adaptation.

We investigate this question for an IVF-inspired operator inside
SPEA2~\cite{zitzler2001spea2} on 51 instances from the ZDT, DTLZ, WFG, and
MaF families, examining static landscape features, early population dynamics,
and a one-shot warmup switch.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item Static landscape descriptors provide moderate prior signal about
	      operator gain magnitude, but not robust cross-family binary
	      decisions: four retained features survive BH correction, Random
	      Forest regression reaches $r_s\!=\!0.374$ and $R^{2}\!=\!0.160$ in
	      LOOCV, and removing structural metadata weakens the signal.
	\item Early turnover is the clearest switching signal in this study,
	      whereas static binary prediction does not generalize across
	      benchmark families
	      ($q_{\mathrm{BH}}\!=\!0.013$, $\hat{A}_{12}\!=\!0.849$; threshold-rule
	      balanced accuracy $0.691$ in LOOCV and $0.754$ in leave-one-family-out
	      refitting, with uncertainty discussed in
	      Section~\ref{sec:early_turnover}).
	\item A one-shot warmup controller based on early turnover that
	      eliminates all hypervolume losses on the 10~\textsc{not\_helps}
	      instances where always-on IVF degrades or does not improve SPEA2,
	      while forfeiting gains on 9~\textsc{helps} cases concentrated in
	      WFG (Section~\ref{sec:ctrl_results}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Relationship to companion work.}
The IVF operator and its empirical validation on this benchmark suite are
presented in a companion paper~\cite{zambrano2026ivfspea2}, which addresses
the design, ablation, and tuning of the memetic hybrid. The present paper
asks a different question---whether static landscape features or early
population dynamics can inform a one-shot keep/drop decision for the
operator---and contributes all landscape analysis, dynamics experiments,
threshold calibration, and controller evaluation reported here. The only
data reused from the companion work are the 60-run IGD results that define
the operator-benefit labels (Section~\ref{sec:fla_setup}).

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{SPEA2}
\label{sec:spea2}

SPEA2~\cite{zitzler2001spea2} is an elitist multi-objective evolutionary
algorithm that maintains a population and an external archive of fixed size
$\bar{N}$. Fitness assignment combines a strength-based raw fitness with a
$k$-nearest-neighbor density estimate; environmental selection retains all
individuals with fitness below~1, applying distance-based truncation when the
non-dominated set exceeds $\bar{N}$. The full formulation appears
in~\cite{zitzler2001spea2}.

\subsection{The IVF-Inspired Crossover Operator}
\label{sec:ivf}

The intensification operator studied here derives from a family of
\emph{in vitro fertilization} (IVF) metaheuristics originally proposed for
single-objective search and later extended to multi-objective
settings~\cite{camilo-junior2011,sampaio2017ivf,sampaio2019ivf,Sampaio2024}.
The operator introduces two mechanisms:
\textbf{(H1)~Dissimilar father selection} selects a fraction $C$ of the
population as ``mothers'' and pairs each mother with a father chosen for high
objective-space distance (top-3 candidates, binary tournament on fitness).
\textbf{(H2)~Collective acceptance criterion} applies IVF cycles
(SBX,~$\eta_c\!=\!20$)~\cite{deb1995simulated} up to
$\mathcal{C}_{\max}$ per generation while the \emph{average population
	fitness improves}, rather than requiring each offspring to outperform its
parent individually.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:ivf} gives the complete procedure.
The operator is skipped once its cumulative IVF evaluation count exceeds a
budget gate proportional to the current global evaluation count, controlled by
threshold $R$. The broader algorithm design and empirical validation
appear in~\cite{zambrano2026ivfspea2}; unless stated otherwise, parameters are
$C\!=\!0.12$, $R\!=\!0.225$, mutation fraction
$p_{\mathrm{mut}}\!=\!0.3$, variable mutation $V\!=\!0.1$,
and $\mathcal{C}_{\max}\!=\!2$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{IVF operator (one generation). Input: population $P$, SPEA2
fitness vector $F$, parameters $C$, $R$, $\mathcal{C}_{\max}$.
Returns updated $P$.}\label{alg:ivf}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\If{cumulative IVF evaluations $> R \times$ current global evaluations}
  \State \Return $P$ unchanged \Comment{budget gate}
\EndIf
\State Sort $P$ by $F$ (ascending); select top-$\lceil C \!\cdot\! N \rceil$ as mothers $\mathcal{M}$
\State Father pool $\mathcal{F} \gets$ top 50\% of $P$ by $F$
\For{cycle $= 1, \dots, \mathcal{C}_{\max}$}
  \State $\bar{f}_{\mathrm{before}} \gets \mathrm{mean}(F)$
  \For{each mother $m \in \mathcal{M}$} \Comment{H1}
    \State Find 3 farthest fathers in $\mathcal{F}$ (objective-space Euclidean distance)
    \State Select father $p$ by binary tournament on $F$ among those 3
    \State Offspring $\gets \mathrm{SBX}(m, p;\; \eta_c\!=\!20)$
  \EndFor
  \State $P \gets P \cup \mathrm{Offspring}$; apply SPEA2 environmental selection to size $N$
  \If{$\mathrm{mean}(F_{\mathrm{new}}) \geq \bar{f}_{\mathrm{before}}$} \Comment{H2}
    \State \textbf{break} \Comment{no collective improvement}
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $P$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Fitness Landscape Analysis for Multi-Objective Optimization}
\label{sec:fla_background}

Fitness landscape analysis characterizes optimization problems through
computable numerical features~\cite{mersmann2011exploratory}. For
single-objective problems, ELA features have supported effective algorithm
selection~\cite{kerschke2019automated}. The multi-objective setting is harder
because the landscape is vector-valued and must capture dominance structure,
Pareto front geometry, and objective correlation.

Liefooghe et al.~\cite{liefooghe2018landscape} proposed landscape features
for multi-objective optimization based on random walks and sample-based
probes, capturing distributional properties of objective values, dominance
relationships, and fitness-distance correlations. Whether such features carry
useful signal for operator-level decisions---rather than full algorithm
selection---has received little attention.

\section{Can Static Features Predict Operator Benefit?}
\label{sec:fla}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:fla_setup}

We evaluate IVF-SPEA2 and standard SPEA2 on 51 synthetic benchmark instances
drawn from the ZDT~\cite{zitzler2000comparison},
DTLZ~\cite{deb2005scalable}, WFG~\cite{huband2006review}, and
MaF~\cite{cheng2017benchmark} suites, with $M \in \{2, 3\}$ objectives.
Each synthetic instance contributes 60 independent IVF-SPEA2 runs and
60 independent SPEA2 runs, with a budget of $10^5$ function evaluations and
population size $N\!=\!100$. All experiments are conducted on the
PlatEMO platform~\cite{tian2017platemo}.

One engineering case (RWMOP9) exists in the broader trajectory batch but is
excluded from the label-based analyses here.

IGD is the primary endpoint for label construction and static modeling. HV
remains the secondary endpoint in the synthetic host comparison and becomes
the primary endpoint in the controller section to avoid reusing the IGD label
metric.

The inferential core of this paper is the host comparison IVF-SPEA2 versus
SPEA2, since the response variable is the marginal gain of the IVF module
over its host. For venue context, exploratory pairwise Wilcoxon comparisons
on the same 51-instance cohort show that IVF-SPEA2 wins 47/51 IGD cases
against NSGA-II and 40/51 against MOEA/D, but only 26/51 against AR-MOEA
and 26/51 against NSGA-III, placing it in a competitive but not uniformly
dominant region. IVF-SPEA2 also incurs roughly $5{\times}$ runtime overhead
versus SPEA2 (median 60\,s vs.\ 11\,s per run at $10^5$ evaluations).
These contextual results motivate the operator-level question but do not
replace the confirmatory IVF-SPEA2 versus SPEA2 contrast.

Because outcome samples are independent (not seed-paired), we use the
Wilcoxon rank-sum (Mann--Whitney $U$) test and the Vargha--Delaney
$\hat{A}_{12}$ effect size~\cite{arcuri2011practical,vargha2000critique} on
the Inverted Generational Distance (IGD) of the final archive relative to
the true Pareto front.
For the binary analysis, an instance is labeled \textsc{helps} if IVF-SPEA2 is
better at $p < 0.05$ with $\hat{A}_{12} > 0.56$ (at least a small effect). All
remaining instances are labeled \textsc{not\_helps}. This yields
41~\textsc{helps} and 10~\textsc{not\_helps} in the binary view (the 10
comprise 6~neutral and 4~hurts cases). These labels are analysis categories
for this benchmark cohort, not family-wise corrected claims of universal
per-instance truth.

\subsection{Landscape Feature Extraction}
\label{sec:fla_features}

We extract 22 raw landscape features spanning five descriptor families:
objective statistics, dominance structure, front geometry, local-walk
behavior, and structural metadata. Representative examples include
objective correlation, skewness, dominance-depth variability,
front diameter, front-gap ratio, information content, neutrality ratio,
fitness--distance correlation, number of objectives, and number of
variables.

Features are computed from 2{,}000 Latin Hypercube sample points and
1{,}000-step probe walks, replicated across 5 independent seeds to reduce
sampling noise. For modeling and visualization, we retain 14 features after
removing degenerate variables and high-redundancy pairs in the
multicollinearity audit.

\subsection{Predictive Modeling}
\label{sec:fla_prediction}

We first assess whether the retained feature sets predict operator
benefit in two settings:

\paragraph{Regression.}
The target variable is the rank-transformed median IGD difference
$\Delta\mathrm{IGD}$ between SPEA2 and IVF-SPEA2, where positive values
indicate IVF advantage.
A Random Forest~\cite{breiman2001random} with 500 trees is trained using leave-one-out
cross-validation (LOOCV). With the 14-feature retained set, the model
achieves Spearman $r_s = 0.374$ ($p = 0.0068$), $R^{2} = 0.160$, and
mean absolute error $= 11.00$ on the rank scale. Under
leave-one-family-out validation, the overall Spearman correlation remains
$0.380$, indicating that static descriptors do capture part of the
between-instance variation in operator gain.

\paragraph{Classification.}
For the binary label (\textsc{helps}/\textsc{not\_helps}), the Random
Forest achieves $\mathrm{BA} = 0.527$ ($\mathrm{MCC} = 0.059$) under LOOCV.
Alternative classifiers score higher under LOOCV (logistic regression
$\mathrm{BA}\!=\!0.728$; RBF-SVC $\mathrm{BA}\!=\!0.690$), but all collapse
to $\mathrm{BA}\!=\!0.439$--$0.488$ under leave-one-family-out validation.
Static features help rank expected gain, but they do not support cross-family
binary switching. This negative classification result is central in this
study: static descriptors alone do not justify a reliable cross-family
keep/drop rule.

\subsection{Correlation Analysis}
\label{sec:fla_correlation}

We compute Spearman rank correlations between each retained feature and
$\Delta\mathrm{IGD}$, applying the Benjamini--Hochberg
procedure~\cite{benjamini1995controlling} at $q = 0.05$.

Four features survive correction (Table~\ref{tab:fla_top5}). The strongest
associations are front diameter and number of objectives (both positive),
followed by dominance-depth variability (negative) and number of variables
(positive).

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:fla_discussion}

Static descriptors carry signal about \emph{how much} IVF is likely to help,
but not enough for cross-family binary keep/drop decisions. Regression
captures part of the between-instance variation in $\Delta\mathrm{IGD}$ and
four features survive BH correction, yet the two classes overlap heavily in
feature space (Figure~\ref{fig:pca}).

Part of that signal is carried by structural metadata (number of objectives,
number of variables). Removing them drops regression from $r_s\!=\!0.374$ to
$0.314$ in LOOCV and from $0.380$ to $0.192$ in leave-one-family-out
validation; classification is weak in both cases.

The static FLA result should be read narrowly. The retained descriptors
contain useful prior information about gain magnitude, but the stronger LOOCV
classification signal disappears in family-held-out tests.
Other multi-objective landscape feature frameworks, such as PLON or Deep-ELA,
remain untested and could change this picture.

Ranking expected gain, however, is not sufficient for an in-run keep/drop
decision. The next question is whether within-run signals are sharper than
these static priors.

\section{Population Dynamics and Early Signal}
\label{sec:dynamics}

Since static models do not generalize for cross-family binary selection, we
turn from ex ante descriptors to warmup summaries from paired trajectories.
The goal is to identify a signal observable during a run that
separates \textsc{helps} from \textsc{not\_helps} cases.

\subsection{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:dynamics_setup}

For the dynamic analysis, we execute 30 paired-seed runs on the same 51
labeled synthetic cases. IVF-SPEA2 and SPEA2 share the same random seed per
run, reducing variance due to stochastic initialization. Each run logs
per-generation snapshots including IGD$(t)$, HV$(t)$, spread$(t)$, spacing$(t)$, archive
turnover$(t)$ (fraction of archive members at generation $t$ that are replaced
relative to generation $t-1$), non-dominated fraction$(t)$,
and IVF cycle count$(t)$. This yields $51 \times 30 \times 2 = 3{,}060$
synthetic trajectory runs. The operator-benefit labels used throughout this
section and the next are those derived from the independent-run cohort
(60~runs per algorithm, Section~\ref{sec:fla_setup}); they are applied
unchanged to this paired-seed cohort. One additional engineering case was
logged in the broader batch but is excluded from the label-based tests.

\subsection{Trajectory Patterns and Self-Regulation}
\label{sec:trajectories}

The IGD trajectories in Figure~\ref{fig:trajectories} exhibit two recurrent
patterns: \emph{immediate separation} (e.g., WFG4), where IVF-SPEA2 diverges
favorably within the first 10--15\% of generations; and \emph{delayed
	crossover} (e.g., DTLZ3), where SPEA2 briefly leads before IVF-SPEA2
overtakes later. These representative patterns illustrate that operator
benefit can emerge at different phases of the run.

\begin{figure}[t]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig2_trajectories.pdf}

		over 30 paired seeds): (a)~DTLZ3 delayed crossover; (b)~WFG4 gradual
		divergence; (c)~WFG8 near-neutral; (d)~WFG2 harmful case. The early-run
		shape of the curves reveals both immediate and delayed IVF benefit.}
	\label{fig:trajectories}

The collective acceptance criterion (H2) self-limits IVF intensity over time
(Figure~\ref{fig:cycles}): early generations typically execute both cycles
($\bar{c} \approx 1.8$), whereas later generations use fewer
($\bar{c} \approx 1.1$). Beneficial instances retain higher cycle counts
for longer; harmful ones taper earlier.

\subsection{Early Dynamics as Candidate Discriminants}
\label{sec:early_turnover}

Archive turnover---the fraction of archive members replaced per
generation---reflects how much the population changes between consecutive
generations. The IVF-to-SPEA2 turnover ratio is ${\approx}\,1.0$ across
instances, so the discriminative signal comes from absolute turnover levels
rather than from the ratio. \textsc{Helps} cases exhibit higher absolute
early turnover than \textsc{not\_helps} cases.

We test 18 case-level summary features with Mann--Whitney $U$
tests~\cite{mann1947test} and BH correction at $q\!=\!0.05$. The set includes
early-run quantities available at decision time (turnover, ND fraction,
fitness, spacing, cycle counts) and endpoint references not available at
decision time (paired IGD/HV deltas).
Table~\ref{tab:dynamic_features} reports the five smallest raw $p$-values.
Both IVF and SPEA2 early turnover survive BH correction, but only the
IVF-side signal is usable without a parallel baseline run.

	\centering

		\textbf{Dynamic feature}       & $\boldsymbol{p_{\mathrm{raw}}}$ &
		$\boldsymbol{q_{\mathrm{BH}}}$ & $\boldsymbol{\hat{A}_{12}}$                     \\

		Mean turnover (SPEA2)          & 0.0017                          & 0.015 & 0.824 \\

		Mean IVF cycles (early)        & 0.098                           & 0.295 & 0.335 \\
		\bottomrule

Among the usable summaries, mean early IVF turnover yields the strongest
signal: $p_{\mathrm{raw}} = 7.2\times 10^{-4}$,
$q_{\mathrm{BH}} = 0.013$, $\hat{A}_{12} = 0.849$, with class medians
$0.250$ (\textsc{helps}) and $0.209$ (\textsc{not\_helps}). A
$10{,}000$-iteration label-permutation test confirms robustness: only 3
permutations reached the observed $\hat{A}_{12}$ (empirical
$p = 3 \times 10^{-4}$; 99th percentile of the null distribution is $0.737$).
Figure~\ref{fig:turnover_boxplot} shows both the label separation and its
relation to final IGD delta. A leave-one-out threshold rule on this feature
reaches $\mathrm{BA}\!=\!0.691$ ($\mathrm{MCC}\!=\!0.311$). An oracle rule
using SPEA2 early turnover reaches $0.704$ but requires a parallel baseline
run.
Under leave-one-family-out refitting, the threshold rule reaches
$\mathrm{BA}\!=\!0.754$ ($\mathrm{MCC}\!=\!0.413$, sensitivity $0.707$,
specificity $0.800$) with median threshold $\approx 0.216$. The signal is
sensitive to the warmup horizon: at 10\% of generations, IVF turnover remains
raw-significant ($p\!=\!0.016$, $\hat{A}_{12}\!=\!0.749$) but no longer
survives BH correction ($q_{\mathrm{BH}}\!=\!0.110$).
Given the modest and imbalanced case set (41:10), this pooled
leave-one-family-out estimate should be interpreted as promising but
provisional.

	\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{figures/fig4_discriminant.pdf}
	\caption{Early-turnover signal on the 51 labeled cases: distribution by

		(right). The separation in the boxplot supports a simple threshold-based
		warmup decision.}
	\label{fig:turnover_boxplot}
\end{figure}

Early turnover thus separates \textsc{helps} from \textsc{not\_helps} cases
more cleanly than static descriptors when the goal is a binary warmup
decision. Static descriptors remain useful for ranking expected gain, but
turnover is the only signal in this study that both survives multiple-testing
correction and supports a simple within-run rule.

\section{Turnover-Threshold Warmup Controller}
\label{sec:controller}

We test a one-shot warmup switch that keeps or drops the IVF operator based
on early archive turnover. Unlike adaptive operator selection schemes, it
makes a single irreversible decision after warmup and does not revisit it.

\subsection{Controller Design}
\label{sec:ctrl_design}

During a warmup phase (first 20\% of generations), the algorithm runs
IVF-SPEA2 and records mean archive turnover $\bar{\tau}$. If
$\bar{\tau} \geq \theta$, IVF remains active; otherwise, the algorithm
reverts to standard SPEA2 for the remaining 80\%. Thresholds are imported
from leave-one-family-out calibration in
Section~\ref{sec:early_turnover} and frozen before execution. For each
held-out family, $\theta$ is estimated from the remaining families.
This yields $\theta \approx 0.216$ for ZDT, DTLZ, and MaF, and
$\theta \approx 0.248$ for WFG. The controller is a single-feature,
single-threshold design with calibration external to the evaluated family.

The controller runs on the same 51 cases with the same 30 paired seeds as the
dynamics batch, enabling three-way comparison (CTRL vs.\ always-on IVF-SPEA2
vs.\ SPEA2). Statistical comparison uses paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests
with BH correction. Case-wise win/tie/loss counts are reported
conservatively: a case counts as a win or loss only when the corrected paired
test indicates a clear directional difference under the case-level comparison
rule used in the released analysis pipeline; otherwise it is treated as a
tie. Hypervolume is the primary controller endpoint to avoid reusing the
IGD-based labels; IGD is checked as a supporting endpoint.

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:ctrl_results}

Table~\ref{tab:ctrl_wtl} reports win/tie/loss counts stratified by the
operator-benefit label from Section~\ref{sec:fla_setup} (labels derived from
IGD in the independent-run cohort).

On the 10 \textsc{not\_helps} instances---where always-on IVF degrades or does
not improve SPEA2---the controller never loses to IVF-SPEA2 (2 wins, 8 ties,
0 losses). On the 41 \textsc{helps} instances, it ties IVF-SPEA2 in 32 cases
but loses in 9, all in WFG. The main failure mode is premature
deactivation on that family. On WFG instances the case-level turnover
distributions for \textsc{helps} and \textsc{not\_helps} nearly overlap
(means $0.225$ vs.\ $0.237$), so the fixed threshold cannot separate the
two classes reliably. A plausible explanation is that WFG's multi-level
deceptive structure sustains high archive churn even when convergence stalls,
which weakens turnover as a proxy for search progress.

Relative to SPEA2, the controller records 34 wins, 14 ties, and 3 losses
in hypervolume (33 of the wins on \textsc{helps} cases). On the
10~\textsc{not\_helps} cases, the controller does not uniformly protect
against SPEA2 (1~win, 6~ties, 3~losses). The IGD summary follows the same
direction but is less pronounced: 41/51 match-or-beat versus IVF-SPEA2 and
47/51 versus SPEA2. Because the label distribution is imbalanced toward
always-on IVF (41/51 \textsc{helps}), raw label frequency alone is not the
right target. We thus read the controller as a risk-management mechanism:
it avoids hypervolume losses against always-on IVF on all 10 instances where
IVF is unhelpful, at the cost of forfeiting some gains on 9 of 41 beneficial
instances---all concentrated in WFG.

\section{Discussion and Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Taken together, the evidence supports a focused claim: for one-shot operator
switching inside SPEA2, warmup dynamics are more actionable than ex ante
landscape descriptors. Static ELA-style features help rank expected gain
magnitude, but this signal does not survive cross-family binary switching.
Early turnover over the first 20\% of generations separates
\textsc{helps} from \textsc{not\_helps} cases more reliably and supports a
one-shot warmup rule. Under family-wise out-of-sample calibration, the rule
matches or beats always-on IVF-SPEA2 on 42/51 hypervolume cases and incurs
zero hypervolume losses on the 10~\textsc{not\_helps} cases. The trade-off is
9~losses on \textsc{helps} cases, all in WFG, where turnover is a weak proxy
for convergence progress. The controller is better interpreted as
risk reduction in a fixed host than as universal protection.

These findings are specific to one operator in one host algorithm, but they
address a practical design decision: whether to keep an expensive
intensification module after warmup. In this setting, static descriptors are
more useful as priors on expected gain magnitude, while early dynamics are
more suitable for the actual keep/drop decision.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our FLA analysis uses ELA-style features; more specialized descriptors such as
Pareto local optima networks~\cite{ochoa2024funnels} or learned features
(Deep-ELA~\cite{seiler2024deep}) remain untested. The sample size
($n\!=\!51$) is modest and class-imbalanced (41:10), which makes threshold
calibration uncertain. The controller uses a single feature with family-wise
thresholds imported from leave-one-family-out analysis---stronger than
in-sample calibration, but not full unseen-domain validation. All
9~hypervolume losses to IVF-SPEA2 on \textsc{helps} cases occur in WFG
(Section~\ref{sec:ctrl_results}); the near-overlap of turnover distributions
on that family limits discriminative power. The 9~losses on \textsc{helps}
and 3~losses to SPEA2 on \textsc{not\_helps} leave engineering margin.
Controller results are endpoint-specific: we use hypervolume to avoid reusing
IGD-based labels, and the IGD summaries are slightly less favorable. The
study examines one operator (IVF) in one host (SPEA2); broader
generalization remains open.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Useful next steps are multi-feature controllers, tests on other
intensification operators and base algorithms, richer static descriptors
(e.g., PLON or Deep-ELA), and functional analysis of full trajectories.
