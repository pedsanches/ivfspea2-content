\begin{abstract}{IVF/SPEA2: A Hybrid SPEA2 Algorithm with In Vitro Fertilization for Multi-Objective Optimization}
This dissertation evaluates whether IVF/SPEA2 improves SPEA2 at a fixed evaluation budget, the source of its benefit, and its activation. Motivated by the hypothesis that SPEA2 density may penalise intensification, the in vitro fertilization (IVF) coupling assigns a dissimilar father to each mother, continues cycles based on mean fitness, and uses a budget gate. Across 51 ZDT, DTLZ, WFG and MaF instances with 60 runs of 100,000 evaluations, IGD as primary endpoint, HV as secondary endpoint, and Holm correction, IVF/SPEA2 records 22 wins, 3 ties and 3 losses against SPEA2 for two objectives, and 15/7/1 for three. On 39 instances not used in tuning, the counts are 19/3/2 and 12/3/0. Across the 51 instances, the median IGD $A_{12}$ is 0.756 and the median relative difference between IGD medians is 1.15\% in favour of IVF/SPEA2; all four losses occur in WFG. On three engineering problems, there is one win and two comparisons without detectable difference against the host. An initial ablation records 50 non-detections and one loss against the variant without the two decisions; historical tuning does not isolate parameter effects, and the confirmatory comparison does not isolate the IVF module. Across distinct pipelines, IVF improves SPEA2 in IGD on 33 of 51 instances, NSGA-III on 22 and NSGA-II on one. A static regression reanalysis with fold-contained response transformation and a joint DTLZ+MaF holdout yields cross-family correlation 0.150. Early archive turnover reaches balanced accuracy 0.754 using a signal and window selected in the source analysis, but instance-level decisions match the WFG-versus-rest partition in 49/51 cases; the per-run controller records 2/40/9 in HV against always-on IVF/SPEA2. The complete IVF/SPEA2 implementation improves its host on most instances of the studied suite; attribution and a beneficial activation policy remain unestablished.
\end{abstract}
