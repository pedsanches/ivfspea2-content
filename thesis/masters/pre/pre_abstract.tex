\begin{abstract}{IVF/SPEA2: A Hybrid SPEA2 Algorithm with In Vitro Fertilization for Multi-Objective Optimization}
SPEA2 assigns fitness by combining accumulated dominance strength with a density term based on the distance to the $k$-th nearest neighbour. This mechanism gives the algorithm a well-distributed solution set, but it penalises exactly those offspring that local search produces, because they are born close to their parents. This dissertation proposes IVF/SPEA2, a coupling of the in vitro fertilization method to SPEA2 that addresses this difficulty through two host-specific decisions: assigning each mother a distinct father chosen by distance in objective space, and conditioning further intensification cycles on an improvement in the population's mean fitness rather than on a single offspring. A cumulative-budget gate keeps the evaluations per generation unchanged, so the comparison is evaluation-normalised. The evaluation covers 51 instances from the ZDT, DTLZ, WFG and MaF suites, with 60 runs per configuration and a budget of 100,000 evaluations, with inverted generational distance as the primary endpoint and hypervolume as the mandatory secondary one, under Holm correction. Against canonical SPEA2, IVF/SPEA2 obtains 22 wins, 3 ties and 3 losses on the bi-objective instances and 15 wins, 7 ties and 1 loss on the tri-objective ones; restricted to the 39 instances not used for parameter calibration, the advantage holds at 19 and 12 wins respectively. All detectable losses fall in the WFG suite; front geometry separates the outcomes across hosts but not within the confirmatory family. Complementary studies bound it: the benefit depends on the host, being far smaller when the scheme is coupled to NSGA-II; transfer to three constrained engineering problems yields mixed outcomes; and the ablation does not separate the full coupling from the variant omitting both decisions, so their justification remains mechanistic. Static landscape descriptors rank the expected gain but do not support a binary decision that generalises across problem families; early-generation archive turnover does, with a threshold rule of balanced accuracy 0.754. The coupling is therefore a bounded, instance-dependent improvement rather than a universal enhancement of SPEA2, with the discriminating factor still open.
\end{abstract}
